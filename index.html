What This Looks Like in Practice

Before

  • Onboarding lacked structure and consistency across teams
  • New hires relied on informal shadowing to learn critical workflows
  • Expectations and success metrics were unclear
  • Managers were not equipped to effectively onboard or develop their teams
  • Leadership time was consumed by repeat questions and operational gaps

After

  • A structured onboarding system aligned to role-specific performance outcomes
  • Clear 30/60/90 day milestones and expectations
  • Managers enabled with the tools and capability to lead onboarding independently
  • Centralised learning infrastructure supporting consistent knowledge transfer
  • Leadership freed to focus on strategic priorities rather than operational firefighting

A start-up with a rapidly growing customer support function was experiencing inconsistent onboarding and increasing pressure on leadership. New hires were taking longer to reach full productivity, and knowledge was fragmented across informal channels.

We partnered with the team to design and implement a structured onboarding and capability framework.

This included:

  • A role-specific onboarding journey aligned to performance expectations
  • A centralised learning system for consistent knowledge access
  • Clear milestones and progression criteria
  • Manager enablement to deliver onboarding independently

Within two weeks:

  • Onboarding was standardised across the function
  • Managers were able to run onboarding without founder involvement
  • New hires had clear expectations and pathways to performance
  • Leadership was freed from day-to-day onboarding dependency

Still Not Sure This Is For You?

Here are the questions I get most often:

Do we need this if we're still small?

Nope. 10 is the perfect size to fix things before they break. It's cheaper, faster, and less political now than it will be later.

How do we know when it's time for L&D?

When you're answering the same onboarding questions twice. When new hires take longer than expected to contribute. When you're scaling but systems aren't.

What if we're fully remote?

Even better. Remote teams need crystal-clear processes and documentation. No more "just ask Sarah" or hoping new hires figure it out from Slack.

Isn't this what managers should do?

Great managers absolutely should. But most are learning management while doing their day job. We help them get the frameworks and confidence to do it well.

How fast can you move?

We work in startup time. Most engagements start with something usable in week one. Full systems typically take 2-4 weeks, not months.

What if this doesn't work for us?

Then we haven't built the right thing. I'll iterate until it fits your team, or we'll part ways professionally. No drawn-out engagements.
